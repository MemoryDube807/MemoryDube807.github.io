Mission origin

The Story Behind the Drive

These pictures capture the beginning of this mission at my former school, Malinga Primary School in Zimbabwe.

Growing up, menstruation was such a strict taboo that I didn't even know what it was until it happened to me in secondary school. The cultural silence was so powerful that, despite living with a loving and supportive father, I was too terrified to tell him. I suffered in silence, using cut-up T-shirts and skipping school out of fear.

But the true catalyst for this project was watching my younger sister experience it. She got her period in primary school and, because of local misconceptions, was immediately accused of misbehaving. I was able to stand up for her, but it made me realize that thousands of girls face that same unjust shame and fear without a protector.

My lived experience, and my sister's, is the heartbeat of this work. We aren't just handing out pads; we are handing back dignity, breaking the silence, and telling every young girl that she is normal, safe, and supported.

Community care

Orphanage Outreach: Rebuilding the Village

I believe that children are the leaders of not just tomorrow, but today. Their creative minds, health, and dreams must be protected. My inspiration for this work comes directly from my own childhood. Growing up in a village, I spent my early years away from my biological parents, but I never once felt that gap. The entire community took it upon themselves to be there for me, proving that it truly takes a village to raise a child.

We still need that village today. No one is an island, especially orphaned children. They need a community to feed them, inspire them, shape their memories, and motivate their dreams. That is why I am actively involved in organizing visits and support networks for local orphanages.

Environmental action

Protecting Our Ecosystem: Nurturing the Nature that Nurtures Us

The pictures above were taken while we were planting trees at Copperbelt University of Zambia.

I hold a simple belief: nature nurtures us. For humans to be able to dream, create, and build, they need a fully functional, healthy Earth beneath their feet, above them, and around them.

Living and studying in the Copperbelt Province, one of Zambia's most heavily mined regions, means seeing the devastating realities of air, water, and land pollution firsthand. The disruption to our natural cycles of life is a constant reminder of the urgent need for climate mitigation and adaptation. This urgency is exactly what drove me to Copperbelt University in Zambia to study climate change and environmental protection.

But while my academic focus is on building technological systems to model and monitor these environmental challenges, I believe true leaders must also get their hands in the dirt. Data and systems are only part of the solution; practical implementation on the ground is the rest. That is why I am actively involved in local tree planting and environmental protection initiatives across the university.

We must protect the importance of every living organism, proving that real change happens when we combine our data with our hands in the soil.
